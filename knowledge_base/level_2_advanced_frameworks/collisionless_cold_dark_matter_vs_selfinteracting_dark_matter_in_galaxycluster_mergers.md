# Collisionless Cold Dark Matter vs Self-Interacting Dark Matter in Galaxy-Cluster Mergers

## 1. Overview
The comparison supports ΛCDM with collisionless CDM as the empirically successful standard cosmological framework, while SIDM remains a theoretically motivated extension with astrophysical constraints but no direct particle evidence. Both share large-scale cosmological successes; their key distinction is whether dark-matter self-scattering alters halo-scale structure, a question complicated by baryonic feedback and uncertain small-scale observations.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
ΛCDM (Lambda–Cold Dark Matter) serves as the canonical model for understanding cosmic evolution and structure formation, positing that the universe is largely composed of cold dark matter (CDM) and dark energy. Collisionless CDM is characterized by particles that interact purely gravitationally and support the large-scale structure observed in the universe. In contrast, self-interacting dark matter (SIDM) introduces elastic interactions among dark matter particles, which can significantly impact the morphology of dark matter halos.

The implications of this difference are evident in simulations and observational data. While collisionless models may lead to cuspy halo profiles, self-interacting models can yield different halo characteristics, potentially aiding in reconciling discrepancies observed in galaxy rotation curves and satellite galaxy counts.

## 3. Mathematical Framework
The mathematical foundation of both models is built upon the same core principles, derived from Einstein's equations and the dynamics of cosmic expansion described by the Friedmann equations. The Vlasov–Poisson system is used to model the dynamics of collisionless CDM, while SIDM incorporates terms that adjust the collision frequency based on the particle interactions, leading to modified continuity and collision equations.

## 4. Skeptical Perspectives & Alternative Hypotheses
Skepticism regarding the nature of dark matter arises from the challenges of directly detecting the proposed CDM particles and the theoretical ramifications of SIDM. Potential alternative paradigms include Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) and warm dark matter models that challenge the frameworks advocated by either ΛCDM or SIDM.

## 5. Verification & Skeptic's Notes
The ΛCDM framework has been rigorously tested with observational evidence from CMB measurements, galaxy clustering, and supernova observations. While SIDM remains a compelling theoretical construct, the lack of direct particle detection signifies a crucial gap. It presents a framework to account for specific astrophysical phenomena but must be reconciled with existing empirical evidence of dark matter's nature and composition.

# Conformal Gravity vs NFW Dark Matter Models in Resolving the Core-Cusp Problem

## 1. Overview
The debate surrounding the viability of Conformal Symmetry Breaking (Weyl Gravity) against NFW (Navarro-Frenk-White) Dark Matter Halo Density Profile Corrections has favorable outcomes for the ΛCDM (Lambda Cold Dark Matter) framework. Despite the theoretical richness of Weyl Gravity, it faces significant challenges regarding unitarity, gauge-dependent interpretations of galaxy rotation curves, and lacks proven success in cosmological predictions. In contrast, NFW refinements—including the Einasto and DC14 profiles—are verified within the ΛCDM cosmology, supported by various empirical astronomical observations.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
Conformal Gravity introduces novel mathematical frameworks but has not achieved broad cosmological acceptance due to its unresolved unitarity issues and complexities. It has difficulty describing galaxy rotation curves in a gauge-independent manner. On the other hand, the ΛCDM model is built on well-validated empirical data from diverse astrophysical phenomena—cosmic microwave background measurements, large-scale structure surveys, and gravitational lensing observations.

NFW and its enhancements (Einasto and DC14) address small-scale observational problems such as the core-cusp conflict in dark matter halo profiles. These refinements recognize that many discrepancies originate from baryonic processes, dark sector interactions, or the evolutionary history of dark matter halo formation.

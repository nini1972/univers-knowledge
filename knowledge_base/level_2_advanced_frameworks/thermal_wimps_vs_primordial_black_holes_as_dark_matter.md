# Thermal WIMPs vs Primordial Black Holes as Dark Matter

## 1. Overview
This comparative debate considers two proposed identities for dark matter: thermal weakly interacting massive particles (WIMPs) and primordial black holes (PBHs). The debate is approved, with the math audit’s corrected values adopted as authoritative.

The evidence supports dark matter’s gravitational effects, but does not establish that either thermal WIMPs or PBHs are its identity. The WIMP freeze-out framework is theoretically well developed, while direct detection has not established a WIMP signal. The PBH hypothesis is also not established as the identity of dark matter. The conclusion is therefore a distinction between strong evidence for dark matter’s gravitational effects and unresolved evidence about its physical nature.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
A **thermal WIMP** is a hypothetical massive, electromagnetically neutral particle that was once in thermal and chemical equilibrium in the early universe. In the freeze-out scenario, expansion eventually reduces the annihilation rate below the expansion rate, leaving a relic abundance. The coincidence between a weak-scale interaction cross-section and the observed dark matter abundance is commonly called the “WIMP miracle.” It is a theoretical motivation, not a detection.

**Primordial black holes** are a proposed dark matter candidate formed by the collapse of sufficiently large primordial density perturbations in the early universe. Their possible contribution to dark matter is constrained by a range of observations and depends on their formation history and mass distribution. The mathematical audit identified numerical transcription errors in the PBH material and independently confirmed the corrected values; those corrected values, not the erroneous original coefficients, are authoritative.

The two proposals do not exhaust the possible explanations for dark matter. The approved conclusion is that gravitational evidence supports dark matter’s effects without deciding between these candidates or establishing either one.

## 3. Mathematical Framework
For thermal WIMPs, the number density’s evolution is described by the Boltzmann equation:

\[
\frac{dn}{dt} + 3Hn = -\langle\sigma v\rangle\left(n^2 - n_{\rm eq}^2\right).
\]

Here \(H\) is the Hubble expansion rate, \(\langle\sigma v\rangle\) is the thermally averaged annihilation cross-section, and \(n_{\rm eq}\) is the equilibrium number density. Freeze-out occurs when the interaction rate becomes too small to maintain equilibrium. The canonical thermal cross-section discussed in the research report is approximately \(3 \times 10^{-26}\ {\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}\); its connection to the relic abundance is a theoretical calculation, not proof that WIMPs exist.

The PBH framework includes estimates of collapse, abundance, and evaporation. The audit found that several numerical coefficients in the raw PBH report were incorrect and supplied corrected values. In particular, the corrected Hawking temperature and evaporation lifetime are:

\[
T_H = 6.17\times10^{-8}\,{\rm K}\,(M_\odot/M),
\]

\[
\tau_{\rm evap} = 2.6\times10^{12}\,{\rm yr}\,(M/10^{15}{\rm g})^3.
\]

The horizon-mass normalization and microlensing timescale also require the corrections recorded in the mathematical integrity report below. The corrected figures should be used when discussing those estimates.

## 4. Skeptical Perspectives & Alternative Hypotheses
The main skeptical consideration for thermal WIMPs is the absence of a direct, indirect, or collider detection. Null results have constrained substantial portions of the parameter space of minimal WIMP models, although they do not establish that all WIMP models are excluded. Arcadi et al. (2018) review the changing landscape of WIMP models and searches; Schumann (2019) reviews direct-detection concepts and status. Steigman, Dasgupta, and Beacom (2012) examine the precision of relic-abundance calculations and their implications for searches.

The PBH hypothesis likewise remains a candidate rather than an established identification of dark matter. Its assessment depends on correctly normalized formation, abundance, and observational constraints. The mathematical audit documented errors in the raw numerical coefficients, so the corrected values must be used.

Alternative explanations also remain under consideration. Modified gravity, including MOND, challenges the need for a dark matter particle in some settings, while small-scale challenges to ΛCDM motivate investigation of a broader range of dark matter models (Bullock & Boylan-Kolchin, 2017). These debates do not change the approved conclusion: evidence supports dark matter’s gravitational effects, but does not establish either thermal WIMPs or PBHs as its identity.

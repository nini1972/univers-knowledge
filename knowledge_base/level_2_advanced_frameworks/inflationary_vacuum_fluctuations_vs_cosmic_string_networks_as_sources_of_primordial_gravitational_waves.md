# Inflationary Vacuum Fluctuations vs Cosmic String Networks as Sources of Primordial Gravitational Waves

## 1. Overview
Inflationary tensor modes and cosmic-string networks remain theoretical, undetected candidate sources of primordial gravitational waves. Observational limits constrain these possibilities, while evidence for the scalar sector and the existence of a pulsar timing array (PTA) common-spectrum signal must not be mistaken for a detection of primordial tensors or a confirmed cosmic-string signal.

The comparison’s skeptic verification score is **5/5**, above the required threshold. A VOS fixed-point equation in the report needs correction; the quoted radiation-era fixed point is consistent with the corrected relation.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
Inflationary vacuum fluctuations are quantum fluctuations stretched to super-horizon scales during accelerated expansion. The observed primordial scalar perturbations are consistent with inflationary predictions, but this scalar-sector evidence does not establish the existence of inflationary tensor modes. The tensor-to-scalar ratio is observationally bounded, with no direct detection of primordial tensor modes.

Cosmic strings are hypothetical topological defects that could form networks and emit gravitational waves. A PTA common-spectrum process has been observed, but its origin in cosmic strings is unconfirmed. PTA and other observational constraints limit possible string-network contributions; the common-spectrum signal is evidence for a signal, not proof of a cosmic-string origin.

Thus, verified scalar-sector evidence and PTA-signal evidence are distinct from detection of either inflationary tensor modes or a cosmic-string network as a primordial gravitational-wave source.

## 3. Mathematical Framework
In the canonical single-field slow-roll framework, the scalar and tensor spectra are

\[
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)
= \frac{H_*^2}{8\pi^2 \varepsilon_* M_{\rm Pl}^2},
\qquad
\mathcal{P}_h(k)
= \frac{2H_*^2}{\pi^2 M_{\rm Pl}^2},
\]

with tensor-to-scalar ratio \(r = 16\varepsilon_*\). Current bounds constrain the tensor contribution; they do not constitute a detection.

The velocity-dependent one-scale (VOS) framework describes a cosmic-string network using its correlation length \(L\) and RMS velocity \(v\):

\[
\frac{dL}{dt} = H L(1+v^2) + \frac{1}{2}cv,
\qquad
\frac{dv}{dt} = (1-v^2)\left[\frac{k(v)}{L} - 2Hv\right].
\]

For radiation-era scaling, \(L=\xi t\) and \(H=1/(2t)\). The corrected first fixed-point relation is

\[
\xi(1-v^2)=cv,
\]

and the velocity equation gives

\[
\xi v=k(v).
\]

The report’s quoted radiation-era fixed point, \(v_r \approx 0.643\) and \(\xi_r \approx 0.252\), is consistent with the corrected relation. The intermediate VOS equation in the report requires correction before its derivation can be considered rigorous.

## 4. Skeptical Perspectives & Alternative Hypotheses
- **Inflationary tensors:** Inflation predicts tensor modes in many models, but observations provide upper limits rather than a detection. The evidence for scalar perturbations should not be conflated with evidence for the tensor sector.
- **Cosmic strings:** A cosmic-string network could contribute to a gravitational-wave background, but no such network has been detected. A PTA common-spectrum signal alone does not identify its source.
- **Alternative explanations:** PTA signals may have astrophysical origins, including supermassive black-hole binaries. Alternative early-universe models, including Loop Quantum Cosmology, can also produce primordial perturbations with potentially distinguishable signatures.
- **Model dependence:** VOS parameters and network evolution are model-based. The fixed point is a theoretical result, not an empirical measurement.

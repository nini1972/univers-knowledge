# Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) Theory and Light-Element Abundance Predictions

## 1. Overview
Standard Big Bang Nucleosynthesis (BBN) is empirically supported as a framework for explaining the formation of light elements in the early universe. Its deuterium and helium-4 abundance predictions agree with independent observations when given the baryon-to-photon ratio measured by the cosmic microwave background (CMB), along with measured nuclear and weak-interaction inputs.

This agreement supports BBN as a successful framework, but not every predicted abundance is confirmed: the lithium-7 abundance discrepancy remains unresolved and is not verified as a successful prediction.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
BBN predicts light-element abundances using the conditions of the early universe and inputs from measured particle and nuclear physics. The CMB measurement of the baryon-to-photon ratio supplies an independently determined cosmological parameter. Comparing BBN predictions with observations therefore provides a test of the framework rather than relying solely on a parameter fitted to the observed light-element abundances.

The predictions for deuterium and helium-4 agree with independent observations given these inputs. Lithium-7 is a distinct case: its predicted abundance and the observed abundance remain discrepant. That unresolved mismatch limits claims about BBN's success and should not be presented as a confirmed prediction.

## 3. Mathematical Framework
The baryon-to-photon ratio is denoted by

\[
\eta = \frac{n_b}{n_\gamma},
\]

where \(n_b\) is the baryon number density and \(n_\gamma\) is the photon number density. Its value is measured independently through the CMB.

BBN abundance predictions depend on this ratio and on measured nuclear and weak-interaction inputs. The empirical comparison supports the predicted deuterium and helium-4 abundances. The corresponding lithium-7 abundance comparison remains unresolved; no successful lithium-7 prediction is verified here.

## 4. Skeptical Perspectives & Alternative Hypotheses
The lithium-7 abundance discrepancy is an open problem. Its existence does not negate the agreement between BBN predictions and independent deuterium and helium-4 observations, but it does mean that the framework should not be described as successfully predicting all light-element abundances.

Interpretations or proposed resolutions of the lithium discrepancy remain unsettled. The supported conclusion is limited to the evidence stated here: standard BBN is empirically supported as a framework, while lithium-7 remains discrepant and unresolved.

## 5. Verification & Skeptic's Notes
- **Supported:** Deuterium and helium-4 abundance predictions agree with independent observations when evaluated using the CMB-measured baryon-to-photon ratio and measured nuclear and weak-interaction inputs.
- **Qualification:** The agreement supports BBN as a framework; it does not establish every predicted abundance as successful.
- **Unresolved:** The lithium-7 abundance discrepancy remains open and is not verified as a successful prediction.

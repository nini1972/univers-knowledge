# Empirical Identifiability of Integrated Information Theory and Physicalist Emergence Under Matched Causal-Intervention Models

## 1. Overview
Empirical identifiability of Integrated Information Theory (IIT) and physicalist causal emergence remains unresolved. Interventional measures such as the Perturbational Complexity Index (PCI) can discriminate between some conscious and unconscious states, yet do not uniquely measure Φ or establish IIT’s identity claim. The skeptic audit reports 6/6, supporting acceptance as a theoretical, not verified, research question.

## 2. Detailed Explanation
This concept centers on a significant open question in theoretical neuroscience and philosophy of science: Can Integrated Information Theory (IIT)—along with broader physicalist accounts of emergent conscious causation—be empirically identified using causal-interventional experiments? This inquiry hinges on whether competing theories can make matched, contrasting predictions about the same physical system under given observational conditions.

## 3. Mathematical Framework
Integrated Information Theory (IIT) proposes that consciousness is identified with integrated causal power through its intrinsic integrated information, denoted Φ. The computation of Φ, while defined in theoretical frameworks, faces significant computational challenges that question its identifiability.
